# Project Guide — Plain-Language Version

This guide explains what the team is making, what is ready, and what still needs
to happen. You do not need a technical background to follow it.

## The short version

The team is preparing a presentation about a **possible** way to notice unwanted
metal in ore and keep a record when an event needs review.

This project is a demonstration—not a working detector. It is not connected to
real sensors, trucks, mine equipment, or automatic shutdown controls. The
readings and records are made-up examples for explaining an idea.

The example journey is:

**A truck passes an example sensing area → sample readings are shown → a
person reviews the example → the review is saved as a sample record.**

## How to try the dashboard

1. Open **Pilot Records Dashboard** in the project preview.
2. Choose **Simulate a pass** to add a made-up example.
3. Select a record to see its sample details.
4. Try **Hold / Inspect**, **Clear**, or **Escalate** to see how an example
   response is recorded.
5. Search or filter the records. Use **JSON** or **CSV** to download sample
   records if needed.

These buttons only change the demonstration. They do not send instructions to a
mine, truck, sensor, or operator.

## The four examples

- **A — Normal pass:** The made-up readings show no event indicated.
- **B — Example flag:** The made-up readings show an event flag for a person to
  review.
- **C — Unclear reading:** The made-up readings could have other explanations,
  so the example asks for review rather than claiming a definite result.
- **D — Repeated examples:** Several made-up runs are shown together. They do
  not prove that a real system would behave consistently.

Every chart and dashboard record is labeled as synthetic. The numbers are not
measurements from the mine.

## The steps from start to finish

### 1. Agree on the idea and safety limits — started

Use the project brief to agree on what the demonstration should explain and what
it must not claim. The team must keep the work conceptual and follow the site's
safety and approval requirements.

### 2. Draw the proposed setup — WP1, not finished

Make a simple picture of the proposed area: where a truck might pass, where
example sensors could sit, and how the process moves toward the crusher.

### 3. Make example readings — WP2, starter ready

Create made-up signal charts for Cases A–D. These help explain the idea; they
cannot establish whether a real system detects metal.

### 4. Show how records might work — WP3, prototype ready

Use the dashboard to demonstrate an example event, a person's review choice,
and a saved record. The current version is only a local demonstration.

### 5. Create the video — WP4, not finished

Write the scene-by-scene story, create conceptual visuals, record narration and
captions, then assemble a 3–5 minute video. The video must show the required
disclaimer at the beginning and end.

### 6. Review and package everything — WP5, not finished

Write short notes about what was assumed, check the safety wording and labels,
list asset sources, prepare the review checklist and stakeholder slides, then
put the approved files into the six handover folders.

## What the final handover should contain

1. **Final video** — the 3–5 minute explanation.
2. **Editable source files** — the project files used to make the visuals,
   charts, and dashboard.
3. **Storyboard and script** — what appears and is said in each video scene.
4. **Simulation notes** — what is made up, what was simplified, and what still
   needs real testing.
5. **Technical examples** — the process diagram, sample charts, sample records,
   and labeled pictures.
6. **Review pack** — the checklist, revision notes, asset-source list, and
   stakeholder slides.

The folders exist, but most of these final items have not been made yet. The
project board lists the remaining work.

## Important limits

- No real sensors or site measurements are connected to this project.
- The demonstration does not prove detection accuracy, distance, or reliability.
- It does not identify the shape of an object or automatically stop equipment.
- Any real filming, testing, or operating response needs supervisor direction
  and Barrick HSE approval.
- Use this exact disclaimer at the start and end of the video:

  > CONCEPTUAL SIMULATION — For technical presentation only. Equipment
  > configuration, detection performance and operating response are subject to
  > detailed engineering, controlled tests and site approval.

## A few terms in plain language

- **Synthetic:** made up by a computer for an example.
- **Operator review:** a person checks the situation and decides what should
  happen next.
- **EMI:** short for a type of sensor that responds to electromagnetic changes.
- **Magnetic / gradiometric:** another type of sensor reading related to
  magnetism and how it changes.
- **G.E.T.:** ground-engaging tools, such as metal teeth or edges on digging
  equipment.
- **HSE:** health, safety, and environment.
- **Work package:** simply a group of related tasks. WP1–WP5 are the five groups
  on the project board.
